Diary № 01 · A Tuesday

Notes on Tuesday

The kind of day you don't remember unless you write it down — so I wrote it down.

Andrew Stuart 11 April 2026 4 min read

Woke at six-fourteen, before the alarm, with the strange clarity of a morning that has already decided what kind of morning it's going to be. The light through the kitchen blinds was the colour of weak tea. I made strong tea, on principle, to balance things out.

The cat sat on the table in the spot I had explicitly told her not to sit. She did this with the air of someone making a stand. I admired the commitment but moved her anyway, and she stalked off as if the entire morning had been ruined by my pettiness. There was, briefly, a small political crisis between us. It was resolved, as these things usually are, by toast.

Mid-morning

A long phone call about a small problem. Then a short call about a large one. Then forty minutes of staring at a single paragraph of a document, trying to work out whether what it said was clever or merely confident. I have not yet decided. I will probably forget to decide.

On the walk to lunch I passed a man on a bench reading a paperback so thick it must have been a Russian. He had the focused, slightly furious look of someone who is two hundred pages in and beginning to suspect the book has more characters than the world does. I felt great fondness for him. I hope he finished it.

Afternoon

The afternoon was the colour of an unanswered email. I will not describe it further out of professional courtesy.

Evening

Cooked something simple — pasta, garlic, the last of the chilli, more parsley than the recipe called for because parsley was on sale and now I have a great deal of parsley. Read three pages of a novel and forgot all of them. Wrote this down instead, because if I don't write the small days down, they vanish entirely, and I am beginning to suspect that the small days are most of life.

To bed at ten-forty. The cat, magnanimous in victory, slept on my feet.
